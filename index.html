THE BRIEFING

A small board.
A quiet threat.

Both players begin with six agents in their home camp. You play Ivory and move first.

  1. Roll, then relocate. A roll of 1–5 lets you move one agent to an empty space with that number. Agents leaving camp must deploy to their own side first. On a later roll, an agent on the battlefield may cross to the rival side. The roll is a destination number, not a number of steps.
  2. Capture or cover. On either side, rolling a row’s number lets you move into the empty space beside an enemy. This does not capture them. They get a response turn to move away, countercapture if eligible, or skip. If both agents stay, on your next turn you may capture in place without another matching roll; that capture uses one action (your turn in Classic). Home advantage: when an invader arrives beside your home agent, you get the first chance to capture without rolling, move away, or skip. If you leave both agents in place, the invader may capture on their next turn without rolling. This applies equally to both players. Use the Capture enemy piece button. If several captures are available, select your agent or choose a target from the list. Red outlines mark a face-off, not an automatic capture; the button explains whether capture is ready or an opponent response is still due. Camp defence on sixes is unchanged. Two friendly agents occupying both spaces in the same numbered row protect each other: there is no empty partner space for an enemy to enter.
  3. Six changes everything. On any turn, before or after rolling any number, you may choose Skip turn and leave every agent in place, even when a move or capture is available. Skipping uses your turn. On a 6, any battlefield agent may return to your camp. Only an agent already on the rival’s side may enter their camp. You cannot jump directly from your home camp to the rival camp. Return home to capture one enemy infiltrator, enter the rival camp and wait, or use an agent already at home to remove an infiltrator without moving. To do that, select your camp and tap it again. Entering their camp does not capture its defenders.
  4. Leave no opening. You win as soon as you have an agent in the enemy camp and no enemy defenders remain there. Watch what happens when your last defender leaves!

Linear mode

Linear has 24 spaces around a square, with your base at bottom-left and the rival’s at top-right. Two routes connect the bases, each with 12 spaces. Numbers run 1–6 outward from each base, with the 6s meeting at each bend. A roll moves one agent that many spaces forward along its route. Leaving your base lets you choose either route. Landing spaces must be empty; you may jump past occupied spaces. Entering the enemy base needs an exact roll. You win by occupying an undefended enemy base, or removing every enemy agent. Keep a defender at home.

Every turn choose a forward move, Skip, or the special for your roll. Specials use the entire turn and cannot be combined with a normal move:

  1. 1 · Step back: retreat exactly one row, including retreating from the enemy base.
  2. 2 · Change routes: move to the empty partner space in the same row.
  3. 3 · Strike ahead: capture an enemy exactly one row ahead in your column; your agent stays put.
  4. 4 · Return home: recall an agent to your base without capturing.
  5. 5 · Short advance: move forward 1–4 rows instead of 5.
  6. 6 · Base defence: use a home defender to remove one enemy in your base.

Linear has no home-side face-off captures, paired protection, cards, or coins. Captures use specials 3 and 6. All three computer difficulties follow the same rules. Games draw after 300 turns.

Ultra mode · Tactics cards

Both sides start with 3 coins and one random card. Each turn brings three new cards for sale. Pay once to buy a card into your hand; playing it is free. Hold up to eight cards, and play as many as you want on your turn. A purchase removes that offer; no shop refresh until your next turn. Unused cards stay in your hand.

Captures earn 3 coins. Ending your own turn with one or more agents in the enemy camp earns 1 coin, including skips. Multiple infiltrators and extra actions do not multiply this income. Coins last for this game only.

  • Reinforcements (6): create an agent at home; uses one action; eight living agents maximum.
  • Second Chance (2): reroll your current die.
  • Loaded Die (4): choose your current roll.
  • False Orders (5): choose the enemy’s next roll. That roll is locked and persists through skips without rolling.
  • Double Agent (5): add one action this turn. Stacks. After your next action, roll again and take another action.
  • Marked Target (3): earn 3 bonus coins on your next capture. Stacks and persists until you capture.
  • War Chest (3): gain 4 coins immediately.
  • Dead Drop (4): draw two random cards other than Dead Drop; needs one free hand slot.

Only moving, capturing, and recruiting use an action. All other cards leave your actions available. Ending or skipping your turn forfeits unused extra actions. An extra action cannot capture a piece you just approached: the opponent still gets their response turn first. The computer uses the same card pool, prices, and limits. Classic has no cards or coins.

Three ways to play the computer

Easy chooses freely among legal moves. Medium weighs captures, protection, and camp safety. Hard also considers your best reply for each possible dice roll. All levels use the same fair die.

Browser edition conventions

The creator’s videos do not cover every edge case. In this edition: agents leaving camp deploy to their home side; battlefield agents can relocate to empty matching numbered spaces on either side; home-side capture advantage applies symmetrically; each numbered space holds one agent; agents paired across the same row protect each other; captured agents stay out; a returning or resident defender removes one infiltrator; a turn with no legal move is passed; losing all agents loses the game; a game is drawn after 300 turns. These are stated implementation choices, not a claim to reproduce the complete printed rulebook.

Tap or click an occupied space to select an agent, then a highlighted destination. Keyboard users can Tab between spaces and press Enter. Progress saves automatically; starting a new game replaces it.
