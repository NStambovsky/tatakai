THE BRIEFING

Two routes.
Make your opening.

Linear and Ultra share the same board rules. Both sides start with six agents, three base health and two Command tokens. Ivory moves first. Ultra adds coins and tactics cards.

Movement and victory

Roll and move one agent that many spaces forward. Choose either route when leaving home. On your home half, land on an invader to capture it. On the enemy half, landing on a defender creates a contested space with both agents alive. The home defender gets the first capture opportunity, using one action before or after any roll. If both pieces stay through the defender’s full turn, the invader may capture on its next turn, using one action regardless of roll. Either agent can move away; that ends the face-off. You cannot land on a friendly agent. You may jump pieces except enemy agents on the outlined central checkpoints (both 6 spaces on each route). Landing on an enemy checkpoint follows the same home-response rule. A contested checkpoint still blocks enemy jumps. Enter the enemy base on an exact roll to deal one damage and immediately return home, even when defenders remain. Reduce its health to zero or capture all enemy agents to win.

One action, or a special

Instead of advancing, use the special matching your die. Each uses one action. You can always skip, before or after rolling.

  1. 1 · Step back: retreat one space; enemy landings follow home-response priority.
  2. 2 · Change routes: switch to the other route at the same distance; enemy landings follow home-response priority.
  3. 3 · Strike ahead: capture an invader immediately ahead on your home turf without moving. Home defenders cannot be struck on their own turf.
  4. 4 · Return home: recall an agent without capturing.
  5. 5 · Short advance: advance 1–4 spaces instead of five.
  6. 6 · Home defence: a home agent captures one enemy anywhere on your half.

Commands, checkpoints and intelligence

Spend a Command token after rolling to adjust the die by +1 or −1, without wrapping. Tokens do not replenish; you may spend both on one roll. Enemy-locked dice cannot be adjusted.

Each side has one intelligence objective on the enemy half, marked ◆ across its two route spaces. Begin your next turn with an agent there and no enemy in either space to collect one intelligence. Extra actions do not collect it early or bypass a defender’s response turn. Each objective can be collected once. At the end of turn 160, if neither base or elimination victory has occurred, more intelligence wins; equal intelligence draws. Normal victories always take precedence.

Ultra · Cards and coins

Start with 3 coins and one random card. Each turn offers three cards. Buy into an eight-card hand, then play or hold them. Captures earn 3 coins; damaging the enemy base earns 1 coin. There is no passive base income. Coins and cards reset each game.

  • Reinforcements (6): spend an action to recruit at home; maximum eight living agents.
  • Second Chance (2): reroll your unlocked die.
  • Loaded Die (4): set your unlocked die to the chosen face.
  • False Orders (5): set and lock the enemy’s next roll. Unrolled skips do not remove it.
  • Double Agent (5): one extra action this turn, at most once per turn. Roll again after your first action.
  • Marked Target (1): choose a visible enemy agent. Capturing it pays 3 extra coins. One active mark; it follows that agent even after movement or recall.
  • War Chest (0): spend an action to gain 3 coins. Each offer can only be bought once.
  • Dead Drop (4): draw two random cards other than Dead Drop; needs one free hand slot.

Moving, specials, recruitment and War Chest use actions. Other cards do not. Skip forfeits extra actions. Winning ends the game immediately. The computer uses the same rules and can spend Command tokens.

Extreme · Three dice and blockades

Each side starts with 6 Agents, 2 Guards, 2 Runners, 5 base health, and 2 repair tokens. Agents use the normal rules. A pair containing a Guard cannot be vaulted, but Guards cannot enter the enemy base. Runners may advance any distance from 1 up to their movement die; they cannot capture, strike or lead an assault, but can support an Agent or Guard in a coordinated attack. All pieces are removed by one successful capture. Read or print the full Extreme rules ↗ There are two routes around the square, each 12 rows long and two tracks wide: 48 field spaces. Roll three dice once per turn, then choose their order. Each die buys one move, special, or coordinated attack. Each agent can use only one movement die per turn. Actions can be stacked with movement or other actions on the same agent. Skip any selected die, or end your turn and skip the rest. Used dice cannot be reused. There are no cards, Command tokens, intelligence objectives, or fixed checkpoints in Extreme.

Move forward by the selected die, choosing either track on that route as your destination. You can weave around a single piece and stop beside either team. Capture a lone enemy on landing. A fully occupied row blocks passage for both teams: ordinary movement cannot jump it. An enemy pair also prevents a lone attacker from landing on either defender.

To break a pair, place two of your agents side by side in the row immediately before it. Spend any one die on Coordinated attack: on your turf, one attacker moves onto and captures one blocker immediately while its supporter stays. If the enemy pair is on its own turf, this first prepares an attack without moving or capturing. The defender then gets a full response turn. If both pairs remain, spend any die on your next turn to finish the attack. Moving a blocker, moving an attacker or support, or eliminating an involved attacker cancels the preparation. Strike Ahead on a 3 also needs this preparation and response when targeting a pair on its own turf; finishing it uses any die on your next turn and leaves your striker in place. Extra dice cannot bypass the response turn. Other specials can help you get around it, but cannot capture a protected piece. A 2 switches to the other route and may capture a lone enemy, but cannot land on a protected pair; a 3 captures either enemy immediately ahead; a 4 recalls home; a 5 vaults 1–5 rows past ordinary blockades but cannot cross a Guard blockade or land on a protected pair; a 6 lets any piece at home spend one repair token to restore one base health, up to five. Each player has only two repair tokens for the whole game. A 1 can retreat one row as an action, or combine with another unused die to reverse by that die’s value. A reverse combination consumes both dice and counts as that agent’s one movement die. Reverse obeys blockades. Route switches, strikes, recalls, vaults and coordinated attacks use actions; they do not spend a second movement die.

Exact entry into the enemy base deals one damage and returns the attacking agent home. Zero base health or losing all agents ends the game immediately, even with dice left. After 160 full turns, an unfinished game is a draw. The computer rolls and spends three dice under the same rules.

Computer opponents

Easy explores legal moves. Medium weighs material, base health, progress and intelligence. Hard also examines the opponent’s replies across possible dice rolls. All use fair dice.

Progress saves automatically in a new Cross Camp save slot. Earlier Tatakai/Crosscamp saves remain untouched but cannot be resumed under these changed rules. New game replaces only your Cross Camp campaign.
